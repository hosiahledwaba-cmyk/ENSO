# ENSO Shield: Claims and Evidence Matrix

This living register tracks all scientific claims, hypotheses, empirical evidence, and engineering assumptions across the ENSO Shield project. No scientific claim may be asserted in the publication manuscript without a corresponding entry in this register.

## 1. Established Scientific Findings (Peer-Reviewed Literature)

| ID | Scientific Claim | Primary Source / Evidence | Scope & Known Limitations |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **EF-01** | El Niño events significantly increase the probability of below-normal austral summer rainfall in subtropical Southern Africa. | Ropelewski & Halpert (1987, 1989); Reason et al. (2000, 2006). | Teleconnection is probabilistic, not deterministic. Certain moderate El Niño events (e.g., 1993/94) produced near-normal rainfall due to regional Indian Ocean dipole interference. |
| **EF-02** | Peak ENSO teleconnection over the Limpopo Basin and South African Maize Belt occurs during the core convective months of December, January, and February (DJF). | Nicholson & Kim (1997); Landman & Beraki (2012). | Early summer (October/November) rainfall is frequently modulated by local Atlantic/Indian sea surface temperatures rather than equatorial Pacific dynamics. |
| **EF-03** | CHIRPS satellite-station blended precipitation records accurately capture drought severity across sub-Saharan Africa. | Funk et al. (2015); Dinku et al. (2018). | Gauge density in rural Southern Africa has declined since the 1990s, increasing reliance on thermal infrared estimates in certain localized zones. |
| **EF-04** | Standard machine learning pipelines are inherently vulnerable to small, targeted input perturbations (adversarial manipulation and False Data Injection). | Biggio & Roli (2018); Barreno et al. (2010); Goodfellow et al. (2014). | Most literature addresses image classification and power-grid state estimation rather than seasonal climate forecasting pipelines. |

## 4. Engineering Assumptions

| ID | Assumption Statement | Rationale | Potential Failure Mode & Mitigation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **EA-01** | Upstream NOAA and CHIRPS HTTP servers maintain backwards-compatible file formats. | NOAA has maintained `oni.ascii.txt` in identical columnar format for $>15$ years; CHIRPS v2 naming format has been stable since 2015. | Format parsing errors are trapped by automated schema validation; automated alerts trigger if file structure shifts. |
| **EA-02** | Regional precipitation can be defensibly represented by a spatially averaged zonal index for initial risk assessment. | The Limpopo / Maize-belt summer rainfall zone shares coherent synoptic-scale forcing during ENSO extremes. | Local microclimatic variation is obscured. Mitigation: PostGIS spatially-distributed grid evaluation is scheduled for Milestone 2. |
| **EA-03** | The local development host (Windows 11, Intel i7-10810U, 48 GB RAM) is sufficient for all proposed CPU-first modeling tasks. | Ingested time-series data consumes $< 100\text{ MB}$; tabular scikit-learn / LightGBM models train in $< 5\text{ seconds}$ on CPU. | Large raster NetCDF operations would bottleneck memory. Mitigation: Server-side spatial clipping and downsampling. |
| **EA-04** | Deterministic test fixtures (`data/fixtures/`) are strictly isolated for continuous integration and unit testing. | Fast, reliable, deterministic CI tests without live network dependencies or multi-gigabyte downloads. | Risk of mistaking fixtures for empirical results. Mitigation: Explicit documentation in `data/fixtures/README.md` and automated test assertions verifying isolation from model evaluation. |
| **EA-05** | Security evaluation harnesses operate strictly on ephemeral isolated dataset copies with zero side-effects on production stores. | Adversarial poisoning simulations must never corrupt golden training records (`data/features/enso_rainfall_features_labelled.csv`). | Accidental overwrite of training data. Mitigation: Ephemeral scratch directory isolation (`scratch/poisoning_benchmarks/`) coupled with cryptographic pre/post SHA-256 assertions. |
